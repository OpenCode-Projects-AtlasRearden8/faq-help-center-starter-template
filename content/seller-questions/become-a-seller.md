We run a small marketplace for homemade and hand-finished goods. Getting set up as a seller takes under an hour; getting good at selling takes practice, and we'd rather help you do it with clear expectations upfront.

## Who it suits (and who it doesn't)

It suits making a modest, real income from things you make or skillfully restore — think a couple of hundred dollars a month to start. It is *not* the place for flipping mass-produced goods: buyers here come specifically for the handmade character.

## The steps

1. **Fill in the seller form** from your account page (email us for a direct link if you haven't got an account — no gatekeeping, just a form).
2. **Describe what you make in your own words.** Photos taken by a window with daylight beat studio photos every time; buyers want to see the real thing.
3. **Price with the fee in mind.** Listing fees explained in plain numbers: [what do the fees pay for?](listing-fees-explained.html)
4. **List a first item and see how it feels.** Most sellers edit their first listing three times; the first one is practice, the second is for money.

## What happens when an order arrives

You ship it within two business days, just like our own shop does, using the discounted postage we arrange. If a buyer goes quiet or a parcel gets missed, you don't absorb the loss alone — read [tracking and late parcels](../orders-shipping/track-my-package.html) and then email us; we back our sellers, which is why they stay.

escape: If you're unsure whether your particular kind of goods fits (a common and reasonable worry — antique restoration, custom sizes, food products all have their own notes), describe them in an email; you'll get a straight yes-or-no with reasoning, not a fortune cookie.
